Coating addresses the pipe surface.

An epoxy coating system applies material to a prepared interior surface rather than installing a separate impregnated liner. Depending on the specified system, application may use brush or other equipment. The condition of the host pipe and the approved use of that particular material are important. A coating should not be presented as automatically replacing the structural role of a missing or collapsed pipe.

Preparation is part of the work.

Deposits, surface condition and access affect whether coating is suitable and how preparation is specified. The desired result and the affected section should be established before a material is selected. Tell us what problems have been identified and whether an assessment record is available. A recurring clog by itself does not show that coating is the appropriate remedy.

Clarify what the proposal is intended to do.

Ask about the system, preparation, application scope, return-to-use requirements and checks included. Drain rehabilitation, cosmetic appearance and drinking-water applications are different matters. We do not claim potable-water approval, manufacturer affiliation, certification or a product warranty without confirmed information. Discuss the specific drain and what the proposed treatment addresses before arranging work.

A useful first conversation

Tell us your city or neighborhood, what has changed and which fixtures or part of the system are involved. Mention an active leak or backup at the start. For planned work, share available layout and product information. Confirm coverage, availability and the proposed scope directly; a request does not establish an appointment or a price.

Common questions

Will coating fix a collapsed drain?

Do not assume that. The existing pipe must be assessed and another repair or replacement may be needed.

How does it differ from lining?

A surface-applied system and a resin-impregnated liner are different approaches with system-specific roles.
