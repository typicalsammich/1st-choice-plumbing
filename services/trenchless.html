Trenchless describes a family of approaches.

Trenchless work can rehabilitate or replace an existing line through selected access points instead of opening its full route. Lining, coating and pipe bursting are different options, not interchangeable labels. The condition of the existing pipe, access, connections and surrounding utilities determines what can reasonably be considered. It does not mean there will be no excavation, no disruption or one method that fits every property.

Suitability comes before choosing a technique.

Recurring backups, deterioration or an identified line problem may prompt a conversation about rehabilitation. A slow sink by itself is not enough to select a trenchless method. Discuss the pipe route and condition, what needs to be repaired, the available access and which parts of the system connect to that section. Existing assessment information can be useful, but a photo of an outdoor access does not establish the entire underground layout.

Understand access and restoration.

Ask where work would start and end, whether any access opening is needed and what surrounding restoration is included. A smaller excavation scope can still require planning around the home and temporary changes in use. Clarify the proposed materials, method and any limitations for the particular line. Do not compare a lining proposal and a replacement proposal solely by price when they address different conditions.

A useful first conversation

Tell us your city or neighborhood, what has changed and which fixtures or part of the system are involved. Mention an active leak or backup at the start. For planned work, share available layout and product information. Confirm coverage, availability and the proposed scope directly; a request does not establish an appointment or a price.

Common questions

Does trenchless mean no digging at all?

No. Access openings or localized excavation may still be needed. Ask about the actual route and access plan.

How is the method selected?

The line condition, repair goal and site constraints should guide the choice. Confirm suitability before arranging work.
