Epoxy Lining

A resin-based liner within a suitable existing drain or sewer pipe.

A liner and a surface coating are different.

Epoxy lining commonly refers to a resin-impregnated liner positioned inside a host pipe and cured to form a new internal lining. This is often discussed as cured-in-place pipe, or CIPP. The specific liner system and design determine its intended function. It should not be described as the same process as applying a thin coating to a prepared surface, nor as a universal fix for any broken drain.

The existing line must be suitable.

Access, remaining pipe shape, bends and connecting branches affect whether a liner can be considered. A collapsed or significantly displaced section may need another approach. Discuss the existing assessment and the section to be treated instead of assuming the entire system needs lining. Preparation and the handling of connections are part of a system-specific plan, not details that can be inferred from an equipment photograph.

Ask about the proposed system and scope.

Confirm which pipe is involved, what problem the lining addresses and how the work affects fixture use while it is performed. Ask which preparations and follow-up checks are included in the proposal. No brand certification, fixed service life or guarantee is claimed here. This page concerns an inquiry about drain and sewer work; it does not claim that an unspecified epoxy product is suitable for drinking-water plumbing.

A useful first conversation

Tell us your city or neighborhood, what has changed and which fixtures or part of the system are involved. Mention an active leak or backup at the start. For planned work, share available layout and product information. Confirm coverage, availability and the proposed scope directly; a request does not establish an appointment or a price.

Common questions

Is epoxy lining the same as epoxy coating?

No. A liner system and an applied coating can have different construction and functions. Confirm what is proposed.

Can every drain be lined?

No. Access and the pipe’s condition and geometry must be assessed first.
