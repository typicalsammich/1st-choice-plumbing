Drains & Pipework

Why Does My Bathroom Sink Keep Clogging Even After I Clean It?

You clean the stopper, the water drains for a while and then the basin starts filling again. That pattern usually means the first cleaning did not address the entire cause. A recurring clog deserves a closer look at where the restriction is and how the drain behaves.

Hair and soap residue around the stopper are common contributors, but a restriction farther along the drain or a shared drainage issue can make the clog return. If several fixtures are affected, stop treating it as a sink-only problem.

Separate a slow drain from a backup

A slow sink holds water while the faucet runs, then gradually empties. A backup may bring water up when another fixture is used or leave dirty water standing without anyone opening the sink faucet. Tell a plumber which pattern you see. Water returning from another fixture suggests a different investigation from residue around one stopper. Stop using affected fixtures if wastewater is coming back into the room.

Check the visible parts without forcing them

A removable stopper can collect hair, toothpaste and soap. Clean only the parts the manufacturer describes as user-removable. Different popup assemblies release in different ways, and forcing the rod underneath the sink may disturb a seal. Keep small parts together. If a stopper resists or you cannot identify the release, leave it in place and describe that when you call.

Repeated clearing may leave a restriction behind

A narrow opening through a clog can allow water to move temporarily while the surrounding buildup remains. That explains why “it drained yesterday” does not always mean the drain was fully clear. The location and condition of the line matter. A professional can consider whether the recurring symptom belongs to the sink assembly, the branch drain or a wider system issue before deciding what work is appropriate.

Avoid adding chemicals to the problem

Do not mix drain products or add one chemical after another. If a product has already been used, stop and tell the plumber its name and when it was added. Standing drain cleaner can expose anyone opening a trap or clearing the pipe to a hazardous liquid. Keep the bottle information available. Do not plunge a basin that contains chemical cleaner, because splashing can cause injury.

What other fixtures can tell you

Notice whether the shower drains slowly, the toilet gurgles or another sink responds when this one is used. Those observations are useful even if the fixtures seem unrelated. Avoid running more water just to test a known backup. In a multi-unit Los Angeles building, let the property manager know if the issue may involve shared drains. Access and responsibility should be clarified before work on shared plumbing begins.

Prevent what you can, document what returns

Use an appropriate hair catcher where the fixture allows it, and keep visible residue from accumulating around the stopper. Do not send wipes or personal-care waste down the sink. If the clog returns, record how long it takes and whether it follows a particular use. The goal is to understand a repeated pattern, not to blame every recurrence on something the household has done.

A drain snake is not a diagnosis

A tool that clears one local blockage does not establish the condition of the rest of the line. Forcing a cable through unfamiliar fittings can cause damage or leave it stuck. Avoid dismantling the trap when you cannot safely contain the contents or do not know how the joints seal. If a previous cleaning helped briefly, report what was done and how long the improvement lasted. That history is useful evidence. It does not mean you need a particular piece of equipment or a specific specialized service; the next step should follow the actual findings.

A few common questions

Is a recurring clog always a sewer problem?

No. One sink can have a local obstruction. Several affected fixtures make a broader drainage check more relevant.

Can I keep using the sink slowly?

Avoid use if it backs up or leaks. Continuing to add water can overflow the basin or expose a leaking connection.
