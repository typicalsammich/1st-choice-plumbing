Why Is My Faucet Still Dripping After I Turn It Off?

A faucet that drips all night is frustrating, especially when you have already tightened the handle. More force is rarely the useful next step. First identify where the water appears and whether the faucet is fully off. Different leak locations point to different parts.

A continuing drip from the spout may involve the valve or cartridge that controls water. Water around the handle, base or cabinet can come from other seals or connections. The model and leak location matter before choosing a repair part.

Watch where the water begins

Dry the accessible area and watch from a safe position. Does the spout drip while the handle is off? Does the base get wet only while water runs? Is there moisture underneath the cabinet? Water can travel along a hose or the underside of a sink before it drops, so the wettest spot is not necessarily the source. A photo showing the whole fixture and another showing the moisture can be useful.

Do not keep tightening the handle

The handle operates an internal mechanism; forcing it can damage the handle or stem without addressing a worn seal. A quarter-turn cartridge is not adjusted like every older compression faucet. Do not assume that a repair shown for one brand will fit another. If the handle has become hard to move or stops in a different position, include that detail in your description.

Identify before buying parts

Look for a brand mark, product paperwork or a model number that is visible without dismantling the faucet. Similar-looking handles may use different cartridges, seals or mounting arrangements. Keep information from the original purchase if available. Buying a generic part before the fault is identified can waste time and leave the fixture unusable if the old part cannot be reinstalled.

Know when to stop using it

A spout drip contained in the basin is different from water entering the cabinet or wall. If the surrounding area is getting wet, stop using the fixture. Close its accessible shutoff only if you know it is the correct one and it operates without force. An old valve that will not move should not be twisted harder. Avoid electrical equipment in wet areas and call for help if the water cannot be controlled safely.

Repair versus a new fixture

A repair may make sense when the fixture body is sound and the correct service parts are available. Replacement may be worth discussing if there are several worn components, significant deterioration or a planned room update. Ask whether the proposed work addresses the visible leak and whether surrounding connections need attention. Appearance alone does not establish the condition of the faucet underneath.

Avoid creating a larger leak

Do not dismantle a pressurized fixture or loosen hoses simply to see what is inside. Work requires a reliable way to isolate the supply, compatible parts and attention to the manufacturer’s assembly sequence. At a Los Angeles rental or condo, confirm permission and access with the responsible person before altering plumbing. A short call with the fixture details can be a better starting point than an incomplete repair.

Keep the leak description specific

A useful inquiry distinguishes a drip at the spout from moisture at the handle or a puddle in the cabinet. Include whether the water appears while the fixture is off and whether only hot or cold use seems relevant. If you have already replaced a part, say which one and when. Do not discard the original parts or documentation before the repair is resolved. Photos and observations can help identify the setup, but the connections still need to be assessed. The goal is to make the service discussion clear enough to avoid unnecessary assumptions.

A few common questions

Does a drip mean I need a whole new faucet?

Not necessarily. The cause, body condition and availability of compatible parts determine the options.

Why does water remain after I shut it off?

Brief drainage from a spout can be different from a continuing supply leak. Note how long the water continues rather than assuming the two are the same.
