Plumbing Repairs

A small leak can interrupt a whole day. Plumbing repairs start with understanding where the water is coming from, when the problem appears and which fixtures are affected. We help with everyday leaks, toilets and household plumbing connections.

When to call

Look for water under cabinets, a faucet that keeps dripping, damp finishes or a toilet that refills on its own. Tell us what you have noticed and whether you can safely stop using the affected fixture. Call to discuss the issue and confirm the next step before making repairs yourself.

Drain & Sewer

A sink that drains slowly is different from several fixtures backing up together. Drain and sewer service addresses blockages and recurring drainage problems, with attention to how the fixtures connect. Repeated clogs are a reason to investigate the cause rather than keep treating the symptom.

When to call

Call when drains gurgle, water returns after clearing a clog or more than one fixture is affected. Stop using backed-up fixtures and avoid adding drain chemicals. Explain what drains are involved and mention any products already used so the work can be planned appropriately.

Water Heaters

Hot water problems can involve the heater, demand in the home or the plumbing connected to it. Our existing service offering includes water heater repair, replacement and installation. The right conversation starts with the type of unit, its symptoms and the space available.

When to call

Notice shorter showers, unusual noises, visible water near the unit or inconsistent temperatures? Have the model number ready if you can read it safely. Call to discuss repair versus replacement. Leave gas, electrical and pressure-relief components to an appropriate professional.

New fixtures can make daily routines easier without changing an entire room. Fixture installation covers faucets, sinks, toilets and related plumbing connections. Compatibility matters as much as appearance: mounting holes, supply connections and drain position all influence what will fit.

Trenchless

Discuss less excavation-intensive options for an existing drain or sewer line.

Trenchless work can rehabilitate or replace an existing line through selected access points instead of opening its full route. Lining, coating and pipe bursting are different options, not interchangeable labels. The condition of the existing pipe, access, connections and surrounding utilities determines what can reasonably be considered. It does not mean there will be no excavation, no disruption or one method that fits every property.

Epoxy Lining

A resin-based liner within a suitable existing drain or sewer pipe.

Epoxy lining commonly refers to a resin-impregnated liner positioned inside a host pipe and cured to form a new internal lining. This is often discussed as cured-in-place pipe, or CIPP. The specific liner system and design determine its intended function. It should not be described as the same process as applying a thin coating to a prepared surface, nor as a universal fix for any broken drain.

Pipe Bursting

A trenchless replacement approach for a suitable buried pipe route.

Pipe bursting uses a tool to break or displace an existing pipe while drawing a replacement along its route. It is a replacement technique, distinct from lining inside a retained host pipe. Access points are needed to carry out the work, and the existing material and site conditions affect suitability. The term does not promise that a damaged section can be replaced without any excavation.

Epoxy Coating

An applied interior coating for a suitable, prepared drain pipe.

An epoxy coating system applies material to a prepared interior surface rather than installing a separate impregnated liner. Depending on the specified system, application may use brush or other equipment. The condition of the host pipe and the approved use of that particular material are important. A coating should not be presented as automatically replacing the structural role of a missing or collapsed pipe.
